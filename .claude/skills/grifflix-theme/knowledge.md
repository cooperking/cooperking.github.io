# Grifflix monthly themes: knowledge base

> Everything learned making Grifflix's themes, kept so each new month's theme starts from it. **Add to it after every theme**
> (what John asked for, what went wrong, the fix): see SKILL.md, step 9. Written 14 Nov 2026 from all the work to date.

Sources: John's messages (7–14 Nov 2026), 15 session summaries, and the dated `(John, <date>: "...")` comments in `_grifflix/web/template.html`. Dates are 2026. Quotes are John's own words, including his spelling. Where a source is only a session summary, it is marked (summary).

## 1. John's taste and principles

**What a theme is**
- It's a "fun frontpage showcase" (8 Nov). Each month is one film-inspired, full-screen, animated scene that you move through day by day.
- The theme is the site's USP: "The difference with Grifflix is that the colour will change every month with the theme. That's the usp of the site - the theme shaping the look" (10 Nov).
- Away from the theme page, only the theme's colour carries over: "we should only keep the bacground colour and only have our own branding - the text should change to a complimentary colour that can be read against it, but the fonts should be the same" (10 Nov). The code puts it as "only the colour travels".
- It started from "what would Stanley Kubrick design a tv app like" (summary, 8 Nov). That became 12 monthly themes: "You don't need to stick to Kubrick for this" (8 Nov 13:25).
- The list can be any length: "I want to be able to add as many themes as I want not just 12" (9 Nov). A theme can repeat in more than one month (9 Nov).

**It must be recognisable at once**
- A theme that doesn't read as its film is dropped or reworked:
  - Psycho: "it's not very obviously Psycho"; "still doesn't give Psycho vibes".
  - Alphaville: "doesn't read like Alphaville".
  - Jaws: "jaws needs shark otherwise it's not obvious what it is".
  - The Shining: "a little abstract. The carpet is the obvious symbol to use to identify it" (9 Nov).
  - Strangelove: "could it be made more recognisable what it is as it's very abstract" (8 Nov).
  - Hugo: "the Hugo theme need something more Meliere to make it clear" (9 Nov).
- Every prop must belong to the film. Kane: "Citizen Kane's baubles are leftover from a christmas theme, but not relevant to the film"; "packing crates aren't right for Citizen Kane, think creatively" (9 Nov). The fix was Inquirer front pages.

**Inspired, not copied**
- Colour should be bold, inspired by the film's design rather than its exact palette: "I still want a bold background color. I don't want his exact color palettes, but inspiration from his design and use of colour and text" (8 Nov).
- One clear colour per film, not a mix: "I don't think it should be a mix of colours, the clear choice from each" (8 Nov).
- Take colour from the film itself, not only from its poster: "Can The Shining font be the one from the film, same colour. They yellow is only from the poster so can we rework the colour again to not have that" (9 Nov).
- Move away from black: "go with other colours to see them, to get away from black" (8 Nov). "I never wanted the default backgroud to Grifflix to be black - that's happened by accident. I like bold use of colour" (10 Nov).
- Black and white is right when the film's look calls for it. The War Room "is really only relevant when it's black and white" (8 Nov). Kane went black and white on 11 Nov ("remove red from calendar make background black").

**Research is expected; don't guess**
- "Are you sure all fonts are actually inspired by the film? Some don't seem to be - do proper research into colours and fonts of the inspired film." (8 Nov)
- "You need to do proper research to check fonts and colours" (9 Nov). "re-do colours and fonts research" (Cinema Paradiso, 9 Nov). "do colour and font research" (Zodiac, 9 Nov).
- Licensed fonts are fine: "Doesn't have to be a free font. Let me know true font and then we work from there" (9 Nov). "I don't mind a licenced font" (10 Nov). He buys or uploads them himself (Futura, Helvetica, Eurostile, Clarendon, OCR-A, Wood Gothic, Kane display, Magneto, Materhorn NF, Archer).
- For colour research, think fresh rather than picking what's obvious or historic:
  - "this isn't research it's cherry picking what's obvious"; "you're using colours because they were used. Think fresh choices"; "I don't care if other people use it. That's the wrong approach" (10 Nov, brand colour).
  - Purple is his least favourite colour. Readability always matters.

**Fonts**
- "fonts for the themes don't thave to stick to the futura if it's not relevant" (8 Nov).
- A theme keeps its film's lettering and spacing: "that futura rule shouldn't apply to themes if they have that specific font - the spacing should match the film style" (9 Nov).
- Futura capitals only when spread like the logo: "futura capitals should only be used if they're spread out like the grifflix logo, otherwise use a different font from the branding" (9 Nov). This applies to Grifflix's own and blank months, not to film themes.

**Movement**
- "Idea is good but movement could be more creative" (9 Nov). The Grifflix theme "the way things move and display should be original and creative" (9 Nov).
- Effects must be real, not faked. On the zoetrope: "If it was done properly the effect would actually be achieve without fading out the posters in and out"; "the spinning is being faked again" (10 Nov).
- Motion should be controlled by the user's gesture, not played as a fixed animation. Jaws: "can the jaws effect happen as the the cards move rather than an animation that plays first, so it's controlled" (9 Nov). The Lumière train: "I'd like the train animated with the swipe" (9 Nov).
- Make things look real, not cartoonish, and not "traced":
  - "curtain looks like a cartoon"; "Lebowki skittles don't look realistic enough"; "stand on the zoetrope doesn't look good - can you find a real reference and use that" (9 Nov).
  - Line-drawing style was rejected twice: "it looks traced, not stylish", then "bad" (9 Nov).
  - "it needs to be done subtley and look real" (10 Nov). "Make it tidy and beautiful" (10 Nov).

**Fit each screen**
- "Every theme need to make use of the screen not look like it's designed for something else and this is an attempt to show it on a small screen" (8 Nov). "Think mobile use for everything" (8 Nov).

**Things he disliked**
- Advent calendar and the first Grand Budapest Hotel: "least interesting" (8 Nov). The Hotel came back later, reworked.
- A title-card transition between months: "The title transition isn't needed" (8 Nov). The month wipe asked for on 11 Nov is different.
- The note "this months theme" under the 1st's film: "it gets in the way" (8 Nov).
- Text on text: "careful that it looks tidy - those screenshots had text on text" (8 Nov). Clashes generally: "be careful of clashes" (9 Nov).
- Flashing or jarring effects: "sunset boulevard flashes in a distracting way" (9 Nov); "Swipes Which rely on animation are jarring" (8 Nov).
- Shiny red velvet: "The only think I didn't like was the shine of the red curtains"; "not red velvet" (10 Nov).
- Two similar themes or colours in adjacent months: "the tron and wizard of oz are very similar styles so put them different months, also simiilar colours of two months shouldn't be next to each other" (8 Nov).

**Process**
- Show screenshots for every design change (standing rule). He decides from mock-ups: "Give me screenshots of your suggestions"; "I need to see them before deciding"; "I'd need a screenshot of each potential look" (10–11 Nov).
- Nothing goes live until he says so: "It looks like things have been pushed live before I was ready... make sure that it's clear in future" (8 Nov). "Themes should be easily saved in order but not publish until I update the site" (9 Nov).

## 2. Hard requirements every theme must meet

### Data and wiring
- Theme entry in `THEMES` or `THEMES_LATER` (template.html ≈ line 2407). Fields:
  - `key`, `mob` (phone crop), `name` ("The X"), `film`, `year`, `by`;
  - `bg` (one flat colour), `ink` ('#000' on light colours, '#fff' on dark);
  - `font` (the film's own face, then a free fallback), `tw` (weight), `tt` (text-transform);
  - optional `credit`/`creditM` for the caption.
- Effects in `THEMEFX[key]`: `decor`, `ready`, `place`, `draw`, `front`, `swipe`, `info`, optionally `push`, `bigSpin` and `cross`. Movement goes through `fxTween` and `vAt`.
- `own: true` shows only the month's own days.
- New theme's film on the month's 1st:
  - Run `_grifflix/theme_films.py`, which writes `grifflix/data/themefilms.json` from TMDB.
  - Admin Save puts the theme's film on the 1st. If Grifflix doesn't have the film, it goes on as a trailer-only `ext` item, with a warning and an entry in the Requests tab (9 Nov: "it should be possible to add it even if the film isn't there - as it should just play the trailer").
  - No duplicate films: admin swaps instead. A film may repeat a year later (10–11 Nov).
- Music: add a pick in `_grifflix/theme_music.py` `PICKS`, then run it to update `grifflix/data/thememusic.json`.
- Licensed fonts:
  - Add an `@font-face "GF-<key>"` with the uploaded file names, about line 1430.
  - John uploads the file on admin's Fonts card. It accepts woff, woff2, ttf, otf and zip, detected by magic bytes, stored in R2 and served from the Worker's `/gf/<name>`.
  - Commercial font files never go on GitHub (gitignored).
  - Google Fonts fallbacks go in `THEMEFONTS`.
- Admin:
  - The theme explorer previews on Computer, iPhone, iPad and TV, with sound (`&sound=1`) and "Play the 3 2 1".
  - The Themes card has year tabs and 12 month slots. You drag a theme from the list, and repeats are allowed. The months each theme is on are listed under it. Save is draft-only; "Update everything" publishes.
- Months from 2026-12 with no theme chosen show `THEMEBLANK` (Grifflix blue, formerly black) "as a holding space" (9 Nov). `BLANKFROM = '2026-12'`. **The next month needs a theme assigned in admin.**
- `?view=themes&theme=<key>` shows any theme, for testing and screenshots.

### Layout per device
- **Desktop:** fill the screen. Strangelove was "squashed to the top in computer" (9 Nov). "it can make better use of the screen in all formats" (10 Nov).
- **TV (Fire TV):**
  - Themes are laid out at 1600×900 and zoomed 0.8 (`TVZ`). "tv tends to be oversized spoiling the theme look, while computer is better - adjust the sizes correctly for tv on each theme" (9 Nov). The TV should look like the computer.
  - Remote-friendly focus. There is one highlight style everywhere: a white ring a little out from the element, a dark band between, with thickness independent of scale (`--ks`) (11 Nov).
  - The chosen day must be obvious: "it needs to be clear on firestick what's highlighted - it's not on Grifflix theme" (10 Nov).
  - No X buttons, no sharing on the TV.
  - "having Grifflix on its own at the top is a waste of tv space" (8 Nov).
- **Phone:**
  - The stage is 780×890, scaled by `themeFit` (`--vk`). Each theme gets a properly designed phone layout, not a shrunken desktop.
  - Examples: on the phone the Strangelove title goes under the table; the moon-phase labels are dropped; the Zoetrope's day words are centred in the space below it.
  - iPhone home-screen app: the theme colour goes right up behind the clock (`black-translucent`); the stage moves down with the bar and caption.
  - No edge line around a theme, because "the iphone cuts off the corners" (8 Nov).
  - The bar must fit down to 320px wide; the profile is never pushed off.
- "Every theme need to make use of the screen" (8 Nov).

### Days, calendar strip and day words
- It must be obvious that each item is a calendar day: "It needs to be obvious that it's a calendar day view on every theme" (8 Nov). Use the calendar-page icon `leafOf()` (`.leaf2`), styled per theme ("the calendar icon for the day could be in the style of the theme too", 8 Nov). The date must fit and be readable:
  - Oz's shadow made it unreadable.
  - Umbrellas' date was misaligned (11 Nov).
  - The date didn't fit the small moon (9 Nov).
- Never more posters than the month has days: "there shouldn't ever be more posters than there would be in that month on a theme" (9 Nov). Strangelove had "far more sitting around the table than in the month" (10 Nov).
- Show the neighbouring months' days as you approach them: "you should be able to see the films in a previous month... coming up, it should just change theme when it gets to them" / "that should be true of all" (9 Nov). The last week before and first week after are shown. Themes with `own: true` are the exception.
- Unfilled days show **Coming soon**, sized to the card with `container-type`/`cqw`: "Can we make sure the Coming Soon fits properly in the photo - for all themes" (9 Nov). Never "Surprise" (Strangelove bug, 9 Nov).
- Day words (date and film title):
  - They sit at a fixed line and don't jump with title length: "Shouldn't jump around. Better to make the title smallere" (10 Nov).
  - Titles are clamped to 2 lines and sized to fit, e.g. "Full Dr Strangelove title just drops down to the next line" (8 Nov).
  - The words must never sit under the bar or under posters: "Could the posters be lower so they don't cover the text" (9 Nov).
- Pictures load immediately, then animate: "Load image immediately and then do animation" (8 Nov). Matrix and Umbrellas were too slow.
- The theme is laid out before data arrives (`themeEarly`) so the calendar never flashes first: "Calendar loads before the theme so looks bad" (8 Nov).
- Posters:
  - Use John's own posters.
  - For wide pictures, use TMDB landscape posters (real titled artwork), not backdrops: "I want my posters but backdrops I Don't like. I actually want a landscaped poster that f the film - real" (9 Nov).
  - Show wide pictures whole, letterboxed, never zoomed or cropped: "don't zoom on the horizontal pictures" (10 Nov); "show the full poster but allow it to repeat" (9 Nov).
- Taps:
  - Tap the middle to open the day's film.
  - Faded or hidden days take no taps (`vAt` sets pointer-events none below 0.3 opacity), after Inception opened the wrong film (10 Nov).

### Caption
- A small caption names and links the inspiring film: "there should be a nice small caption linking to the film that inspired it" (8 Nov).
- It sits on its own panel ("theme text should maybe have a background to distinguish it").
- It's kept away from the day's film title so it never looks like the selected film (8 Nov), and is bigger on mobile ("Caption about film is too small on mobile").
- It can move corner per theme. For Grifflix's own theme it carries the credits (9–10 Nov).
- It isn't shown on the calendar: "we don't need the theme caption on the calendar" (10 Nov). This reverses the 9 Nov ask for a caption with ‹ › arrows on the calendar.

### Bar, buttons and navigation
- The bar is the same in every view and format, with words always in one place: "Search theme/ calendar should always be in the same place no matter what the format" (8 Nov).
- Calendar and Theme are one square toggle (`.vtog`): "calendar and theme should be a toggle as you only see one or the other" (10 Nov); "the theme calendar toggle is rounded" was a fault (11 Nov).
- GRIFFLIX logo: reloads and keeps your place (page, month, day, pop-up). A home icon beside it goes to the current month. No separate reload button.
- Profile always at the right (10 Nov).
- The mute button is in the top bar, on the theme page only (11 Nov). It must not cover the profile on small phones (11 Nov).
- The calendar is full screen over the theme. It opens from the toggle in the month's style, with titles in boxes like the SOTD calendar ("calendar is going in front of theme but not covering it so it looks odd", 8 Nov).
- On TV: remote arrows on the calendar arrows keep focus on the arrows, never via the 1st (8 and 11 Nov).
- Day pop-ups keep the theme's style (8 Nov).
- Future months stay hidden unless John's admin "Show upcoming films" is on. Don't reveal upcoming films to John in messages either.

### Input (swipe, wheel, remote)
- **Phone:**
  - Swipe up or left for the next day, down or right for the previous, whichever way the finger first goes: "Left and right feel the right thing to do for me, so maybe both" (8 Nov).
  - Everything moves together: "Everything should scroll at once. At the moment on some the text does and image doesn't catch up" (8 Nov).
  - A day steps the moment the finger has gone a little way, then another each third of the screen. A flick runs on a few more days, slowing.
  - One ordinary swipe is one day: "The theme scroll is going from today to the day before yesterday" (12 Nov) was the bug that set this.
  - Pull-to-refresh is blocked inside the theme; the logo reloads instead.
- **Computer:** a two-finger trackpad swipe or a mouse wheel moves one day per step, anywhere on the theme (8 Nov: "a swipe function for chrome app, with touch pad too"). Continuous trackpad steps must be smooth, not re-pushed each step (Inception jitter, 11 Nov).
- **TV:** remote arrows; Back steps back a level; tab order "remote control intuitive" (10 Nov).

### Transitions between months
- Crossing happens the instant the scroll leaves the 1st or last day, never after it stops: "Change the moment the scroll leaves the first of the month into the last month, or the moment it leaves the last day of the moneth into the new month" (9 Nov).
- Never flip back to the month just left ("can sometimes cause it to change back to the other theme unexpectedly", 9 Nov).
- A big swipe begun inside the month stops at its 1st or last day; another swipe from there goes on into the next month: "Can a big swipe go no further than the first of that month" (11 Nov).
- The wipe transition is a panel in the **destination** month's colours (its ground, edged in its ink): "can a nice transition between theme be introduced, like a wipe"; "Can wipe transition match the coming/ previous month" (11 Nov). With reduced motion, a fade instead.
- Going back past the 1st must change to the previous month's theme (10 Nov).

### Music
- Each theme plays a 30-second preview of the film's **most recognisable** piece and part: "Make sure the most recognisable part from the film is playing, otherwise it's strange" (9 Nov). For the Matrix: "can the well known part of the song be playing as a preview - not the bit that is playing" (10 Nov).
- It plays once, faded in and out.
- It stops when:
  - a film is chosen;
  - the calendar, Search or Browse is open;
  - a film or trailer plays;
  - a whole-screen page covers the theme;
  - the page is hidden or the app left. The stop must be immediate, because a fade's frames stop with the page: "The music carries on when you leave the app too - which is not good" (10 Nov).
- It must not stutter: preload before starting, which matters on Fire Stick ("sound is slow to load on a firestick", 9 Nov).
- On iPhone web, muted by default, because nothing plays before a touch (9 Nov). An iPhone needs playback to start, muted, to keep loading.
- Controls: a mute toggle in the top bar, and "Theme music" on/off on the profile, with no long explanatory text ("remove this from profile", 9 Nov).
- Admin preview plays the music.

### Performance (Fire TV)
- "fire tv is really struggling with the themes - there's no smoothness to the movements - it must be possible to speed it up without losing quality" (10 Nov). Also "Inception felt particularly clunky but zoetrope not great either" (9 Nov).
- Rules that worked:
  - Draw complex scenes on one canvas, not hundreds of DOM shapes remade per frame.
  - Bake static layers.
  - Stamp repeated ornaments from a pre-drawn sprite.
  - Hide off-screen posters rather than move them each frame.
  - On the TV: use dpr 1, and redraw heavy parts every other frame.
  - Keep full resolution where it shows: half-res Inception was "low quality" on the TV (9 Nov).
  - Pause the theme while a page covers it (`html.covered`).
- Test with Playwright at 6× CPU throttle and the TV user agent (about 34–40 fps achieved on the drum). Now: `node _grifflix/themetools/perf.js <key>`, which checks the budget.
- 14 Nov: "The dr strange love is noticeably heavier than the rest" ("by feel on iPhone and visibly slower on fire tv"). Measured:
  - Its ~30 posters were moved by `left`/`top`, so the page was laid out again every frame (183 layouts for six moves).
  - The Big Board's bomber paths were a CSS `stroke-dashoffset` keyframe animation, which redrew the whole dotted map SVG every frame even when untouched: about 9× the idle work of other themes.
  - Fix: posters moved by `transform: translate() scale()` only; the board split into a still SVG and a small moving SVG, each on its own layer (`will-change`); the paths ticked on by a 250 ms timer (paused when hidden, off with reduced motion). Rules now in SKILL.md, Performance.
- Baseline, 14 Nov (perf.js, TV, 6× slower), work while idle / layouts in six moves: corridor .007/6, slices .002/189, umbrellas .005/11, code .020/6, spiral .007/6, tron .009/12, pinboard .004/6, moon .005/162, reel .008/6, oz .008/6, table .126/6, **globe 2.56/127 (heaviest by far: its snow canvas runs constantly; 34 fps moving)**. Slices and moon still lay out every frame while moving, without dropping frames yet.
- 14 Nov, Kane's globe fixed (John: "It's on December for last year so should be fixed"): snow drawn at the screen's own size (dpr 1) and 30 times a second (20 on a TV), each flake size drawn once and copied (`flakeAt`), its canvas size checked once a second, nothing drawn while the page is hidden; the globe placed on the picture now and then rather than every step (`fit()` measured the page each step); the shake by `element.animate` rather than a class taken off and on (`void offsetWidth` laid the page out each day). Result: idle 2.56 → about 1.1–1.3 s, layouts in six moves 127 → 13, 34 → 53+ fps moving. Falling snow is a canvas drawn constantly, so a snow theme stays above the idle budget: keep flakes few, never more than one full-screen snow layer per scene if building a new one, and consider pre-rendered snow tiles moved by `transform` (no per-frame drawing) for any new snow.

### Reduced motion and accessibility
- `CALM` (`prefers-reduced-motion`): tweens are instant, the wipe becomes a fade, and decorative CSS animations stop (twinkles, Big Board paths, flicker, grain, projector).
- Ink must be readable: check WCAG contrast for every pairing (done for brand colours: cream on blue 9.6:1, orange accent 4.7:1).
- Decorative leaf icons are `aria-hidden`.

### Logo, branding, sharing
- GRIFFLIX bar logo:
  - Currently Futura bold spaced capitals, see-through with a white line round each letter, "like vertigo" (10 Nov, after a Grifflix Display cut-out version).
  - It is in the month's ink.
  - "the old fashioned logo clashing with a new look" (10 Nov).
- Grifflix's own type: Grifflix Display (made from Federo) for titles, Libre Caslon to read, Futura for the logo, menu and buttons. Film themes keep their own lettering.
- Share cards (1200×630) and share or landing pages use Grifflix branding, with only the month's colour from the theme (10 Nov).
- Sharing is available for a theme, a calendar and a film, but not on the TV.
- Invites have no theme, "because you can't see them" (8 Nov).
- No visible "Song of the Day" anywhere in Grifflix. No SOTD styling should flash while loading (8 Nov).
- Signed-out visitors may see the themes and the calendar's films (10 Nov).

### The 3-2-1 leader (Grifflix's own opening, not part of a theme)
- "Should be separated as a loader anyway. Not part of the theme" (9 Nov).
- At most once every 3 hours, and when returning to an app left open.
- Never over a film. Held until the Mac answers.
- Picture and sound share one recorded track's clock, so the beeps play on iPhone and stay in sync on TV.
- GRIFFLIX glides into the bar logo and fades into the theme. Admin and profile have "Play the 3 2 1".

## 3. Mistakes made and corrections (what went wrong → the rule now)

### Process
1. Kubrick look pushed live unintentionally (8 Nov: "things have been pushed live before I was ready") → Draft first, then preview at grifflix.co.uk/preview. Go live only when John says. Admin theme Save is draft-only. Label clearly what goes live.
2. Cinema Paradiso was silently swapped for Sherlock Jr. John noticed: "I feel like it was originally Cinema Paradiso and that got replaced" (9 Nov), and asked "were any other themes changed to a different film" → Never change a theme's film without asking.
3. Fonts claimed to be "inspired by the film" weren't. On 8 Nov research found only three holding up (Matrix, roughly Hugo and Late Autumn), and five backgrounds not from the film at all (Sherlock Jr. red, Hugo navy, Alphaville blue, Kane green, All the President's Men cork) → Research every font and colour before building, and state confidence.
4. Shining colour taken from Saul Bass's poster yellow → Colours come from the film's frames or production design, unless the poster is the film's defining image.
5. Colour work based on history or "distinctiveness" → John: "cherry picking what's obvious", "using colours because they were used", "I don't care if other people use it" → Propose fresh options as mock-ups. No purple. Check readability. He may supply the palette himself (he did: Orange #FF8400, Vista Bleu #8FA0D8, Amande #F9DFC6, Bleu Oxford #0B0829; he finally chose poster blue #173a5e).
6. Misreading his wording, 10 Nov (summary):
   - "over the drum" meant hovering above it in 3D;
   - "come down a bit lower" didn't mean move it left;
   - "Screen didn't need to get bigger";
   - "I just wanted the projector lighting on, not the lights down effect";
   - "you've made it a black background again";
   - "Posters and lettering on the paper strip" when he meant "the red strip on the outside".
   → Change only what he named. Ask or mock up when unsure.
7. Twice telling John he had interrupted when it was an automated stop → Never blame the user.
8. Stray screenshots left in the repo, and an accidental worktree commit → Keep screenshots in the scratchpad.
9. Inline `//` comments placed mid-expression broke JS twice: the big spin broke ("rf is not defined"), and `.sort().map` was cut off. A partial removal left an unclosed `@media (prefers-reduced-motion){` that broke all later CSS → Put comments on their own line. Brace-balance check after edits.
10. Signed-out-only tests missed the signed-in Search button that pushed the profile off small phones → Test signed in and signed out, at many widths (240–768).

### Recognisability and concept
11. Psycho "The Bars" didn't read as Psycho → parked, replaced by Oz.
12. Alphaville didn't read → parked, reworked as "Alpha 60".
13. Jaws: the drawn shark looked strange; "a fin is enough"; "that isn't what a shark fin looks like"; "rework jaws completely and pick another theme" (8 Nov) → parked as "The Deep" (no shark), Tron in its place. Later it was rebuilt from John's own 12-frame attack image. Rule: don't hand-draw iconic creatures; use real reference frames.
14. Shining too abstract → the Hicks hex carpet. Then Room 237 at the corridor's end "looks bad. It's a clash of different styles" → removed. Then the drawn corridor was "too wide and not like the picture" → John's own photo of the corridor.
15. Strangelove table too abstract → the Big Board wall map with bombers' paths, and the ring of lamps over the table. The halo had cut across the top bar → kept under the bar.
16. Kane baubles (Christmas leftovers), then packing crates → Inquirer front pages spinning in like the headline montages.
17. Hugo not Méliès enough → the real public-domain Méliès moon frames: the face, then the rocket landing in his eye on each new day, cropped round the moon, not a circle.
18. Advent calendar and Grand Budapest "least interesting" → removed. The Hotel was later rebuilt around Mendl's boxes.

### Fonts and lettering
19. Futura used everywhere → Theme fonts are per film. Futura capitals only when spread like the logo.
20. Applying the Futura rule globally broke film themes' letter-spacing → The rule is scoped to Grifflix's own theme and blank months only.
21. Hugo's IM Fell "looks wrong" → Cinzel 700. The HUGO logo face (possibly University Roman Bold) is unconfirmed.
22. Reel "Font isn't right" → research found Fanfare (1927), revived as Materhorn NF (a visual match). Titan One is the fallback.
23. Lebowski "font is wrong" → Magneto (Fonts In Use, opening titles; John's choice). The upload "MAGNETOB.TTF ... isn't a font" → the Fonts card now accepts .ttf and .otf.
24. Font zip upload failed:
    - "failed to open", then "Failed to fetch": CORS blocked the X-Font-Name header → name passed as a query parameter and the header allowed.
    - John couldn't see the zip in the Chrome app → files sent separately.
25. Vertigo lettering: "Can Vertigo have the clear lettering with a white border that you can see in the credits" → transparent fill with a white stroke (Special Gothic Expanded One). No source names the credits face; John was told.
26. Header letter-spacing wasn't like GRIFFLIX → match the logo's tracking where Futura is used.

### Colour

## 5. Research method that worked

**Fonts and lettering**
- Check the actual title frames and posters, not memory. Sources used:
  - Fonts In Use (Lebowski Magneto, Grand Budapest Archer).
  - Art of the Title (Shining Helvetica; Zodiac cipher; Psycho "sans-serif").
  - Typeroom (Psycho faces).
  - AIGA Eye on Design (Palladino's Psycho logo).
  - Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons scans; IMP Awards and originalfilmart (Cinema Paradiso one-sheets).
  - Secondary design write-ups quoting *The Art of Tron*.
  - Font-ID sites like Fontmeme (Hugo; low confidence).
- Report confidence honestly: say when a face is custom lettering (Bass's Shining poster, Tron logo, Psycho logo, Annie Atkins's sign) or only a visual match (Fanfare/Materhorn for Cinema Paradiso). John prefers "Let me know true font and then we work from there".
- Lay the candidate font next to crops of the original lettering and compare by eye. Confirm Google Fonts availability and weights for fallbacks.
- When the true font is commercial, name it and John buys or uploads it. Keep a free fallback in the font stack until the upload exists.

**Colours**
- Sample hex from frames or scans and label them as sampled, not exact. Distinguish film colours from poster colours. Pick one clear colour per film.
- Check contrast (WCAG) for ink and accents. Alternate light, mid and dark across months, and keep similar styles or colours out of adjacent months.
- For brand colour, John rejected research-by-precedent. What worked was mock-ups of fresh options, then his own palette and choice.
- Notes from the colour research live in `/home/user/cooperking.github.io/research_notes/Grifflix brand colour research/` and `/home/user/cooperking.github.io/reports/Grifflix brand colour research.md` (git-excluded).

**Imagery**
- Use real reference material, not drawings, for anything iconic:
  - the Méliès moon (public-domain Internet Archive copy; frames pulled with ffmpeg);
  - Lumière's train (public domain, 40-frame sprite);
  - Muybridge's horse;
  - the zoetrope pedestal (from a real reference photo);
  - John's own Shining corridor photo, Kane hand image and Jaws frames.
- Wikimedia blocks generic user agents, so a descriptive UA is needed. Version asset URLs (`?v=N`) when replacing images.
- Collection covers (related work) were researched per film ("Do more research for character images before choosing again", 8 Nov).

**Music**
- Choose the piece the film is remembered by, and the release whose 30-second preview is the famous part.
- Source: Apple iTunes Search API previews (Spotify no longer gives previews) via `_grifflix/theme_music.py`, matching artist, title and optionally album.
- The Worker `/gm/<key>-<h>.m4a` fetches the preview once into R2 (sha256-prefix check), serves it from the edge cache with Range support, and keeps the clips off GitHub.
- Licensing caveat: these are store previews cached for a private family site.
- Grifflix's own background music is public domain (FreePD).

**Licensing**
- Commercial fonts (Futura, Helvetica, Eurostile, Clarendon, OCR-A, Wood Gothic, Magneto, Archer) and Materhorn NF ("freeware but not to be redistributed") are John's own copies. They are uploaded via admin, served by the Worker only to Grifflix's origin, and never committed.
- Grifflix Display is derived from Federo (OFL); its licence file is shipped.

**Verification**
- Use Playwright screenshots of `?view=themes&theme=<key>` on desktop (1440×900), iPhone emulation (including standalone and safe-area), TV user agent, and widths 240–768. Run them signed in and signed out.
- Tap-test every theme; check zoetrope reversals and landings; run perf at 6× CPU throttle with the TV user agent.
- Send John the screenshots before he judges.
- Workflow: `python3 _grifflix/web/page.py`, then commit the template plus the draft, admin.html and themes.json, then PR, squash merge, check the preview. Go live only on his word.
