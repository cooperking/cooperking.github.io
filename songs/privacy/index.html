Privacy

John’s Song of the Day · last updated 27 October 2026

John’s Song of the Day is a personal calendar of songs, one for each day, made by John Griffiths. It’s on the web at cooperking.github.io/songs, and the iPhone, Android and Fire TV apps open that same calendar, so this page covers all of them. There are no adverts and no tracking or analytics.

Visitors

Anyone can use the calendar without signing in. Nothing about you is kept by the calendar: the songs you add to “My playlist”, and its name, are saved only on your own device.

Members

Members are people John invites, who sign in with an invite link or a one-use code. For them, the calendar’s small service (on Cloudflare, at sotd-playlists.cooperking.workers.dev) keeps:

  • your name, as John entered it when he invited you;
  • for each device you sign in on: a one-way scrambled copy of its sign-in key (the key itself stays on the device), a random number the device made to tell itself apart, and when it signed in and was last used;
  • the playlists you save, with their names, so all your devices have them;
  • your inbox: the latest 60 messages sent to you (each day’s song, and John’s own), and which you’ve read;
  • what you’re playing, so another of your devices can carry on with it: the song, where it has got to, the playlist it’s in, and which kind of device it’s on (iPhone, Mac…) with that device’s random number. It’s replaced each time that changes, cleared when you press Stop, and gone after a week;
  • if you turn notifications on: the address your device’s notification service gives for that device (Apple’s, Google’s, Mozilla’s or Microsoft’s), through which each notification is delivered;
  • if you add the iPhone app’s “On your other device” widget (iOS 26 or later): the address Apple gives that widget, so the service can tell it to look again when what you’re playing changes. Nothing is shown by it. It’s forgotten when that iPhone signs out.

Songs you download to play offline are kept only on your device. John can see members’ names, how many devices each has signed in and when one was last used, and whether notifications are on; no one else can see any of this.

Signing out on a device removes its sign-in and its downloads. When John removes a member, their record, saved playlists, inbox, what they were playing and notification and widget addresses are deleted.

Stopping misuse

To stop guessing of codes, the service keeps the internet address of a device that enters wrong codes, for up to two hours. To stop misuse of “Open in Spotify”, it counts the playlists made from each internet address, for an hour.

The apps

The iPhone app is shared through Apple’s TestFlight: Apple passes on crash reports, and any feedback you choose to send, to John, under Apple’s privacy policy. Its notifications come through Apple’s notification service. For its “On your other device” widget, a member’s sign-in key is kept on the iPhone, in the keychain the app and its widgets share, and taken back when they sign out. The Android and Fire TV apps keep nothing beyond what’s described above.

Other services the calendar uses

Song previews and album covers come from Spotify. A song’s video plays in YouTube’s own player, which is loaded only once you play a video. Fonts come from Google Fonts. The site itself is served by GitHub Pages, and the members’ service by Cloudflare. Opening a list “in Spotify” sends its songs’ Spotify IDs, and its name, to the calendar’s service, which makes the playlist on Spotify. Each of these services handles what it serves under its own privacy policy.

The Song of the Day Google app

The “Song of the Day” app on Google is used only by John’s own automation. It searches YouTube for each day’s song and keeps the Song of the Day playlist on YouTube up to date. It acts only for the YouTube channel John signed in with, and reads or keeps no one else’s data.
